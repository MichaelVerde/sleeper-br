Ten weeks in, Tom had remembered how to play football, the playoff bubble was turning into a knife fight, and Holden was somehow the hottest manager in the bottom half of the standings.

Meanwhile, one manager scored 69 points in a must-win matchup, another left 35 quarterback points on the bench, and Rob continued proving that you do not actually need to be good at fantasy football to win.

## The Matchups

### Roop 99.32, Aron 60.92

Roop improved to **9–1**, extending the winning streak to four games without even cracking 100 points.

Aron delivered the week’s lowest score, but the real achievement was starting a quarterback who scored **3.02 points while leaving a 35.42-point performance on the bench**.

That was 32.40 points of potential improvement at one position. It still would not have been enough to win, but at least the defeat could have looked like an actual football game.

Roop showed up with a butter knife. Aron brought a spoon, dropped it, and stabbed himself in the foot.

---

### Alastair 138.16, Jimmer 101.62

Jimmer’s four-game winning streak was dead.

Alastair dropped 138.16, led by a 33.16-point quarterback and another 24.30-point performance, to improve to 6–4.

Jimmer had two starters combine for **2.50 points**, and a recently acquired quarterback scored 15.38 on the bench while the starter scored 34.42. At least the quarterback decision was correct.

Jimmer spent four weeks convincing everyone he was a championship contender, then showed up to the most important part of the season with half his roster apparently working from home.

Alastair was now one of the most dangerous managers in the playoff race.

---

### Griff 141.86, Mark 132.98

The week’s best matchup.

Griff scored 141.86, with seven starters reaching double digits, and survived Mark’s 132.98-point performance by just **8.88 points**.

Mark had a 38-point explosion and a 23.20-point performance, but his quarterback scored only 9.98 while a 26.78-point alternative sat on the bench. That was a 16.80-point quarterback difference in a matchup decided by 8.88.

A potential win was sitting on the bench while Mark watched his playoff hopes take another beating.

Griff was 7–3 and preparing for the playoffs. Mark was 3–7 and preparing an appeal to the fantasy gods.

---

### Tom 152.06, Gav 95.84

The recession was over.

After consecutive 79-point disasters, Tom responded with **152.06**, the highest score of Week 10.

Four starters scored at least 20, including a 31.70-point performance, while Minnesota’s defence contributed another 20.

Gav scored 95.84 despite starting a player who contributed 0.30 points.

Tom’s 965 FAAB investment had recovered. Unfortunately for Gav, the economic stimulus arrived in the form of a 56.22-point beating.

The former undefeated manager was 8–2 and back in the championship conversation.

---

### Holden 110.90, Verde 98.96

The doorbell was on a winning streak.

After losing seven straight, Holden had won two consecutive games. A 32.10-point performance and another 21.70 carried him past Verde, whose 40.70-point explosion from one starter was almost half his entire offensive production.

Verde had five other starters combine for just **16.10 points**.

Imagine getting 40 points from one player and still losing to a building that had been declared uninhabitable six weeks earlier.

Holden was now 3–7. The house still had no roof, but apparently the electrical system had been restored.

---

### Rob 81.90, Jaynaan 69.24

The week’s most offensive football game—not offensive as in high-scoring, but offensive as in a crime against the sport.

Rob won with **81.90 points** despite starting a player who scored zero and getting 44.30 from a single player, more than half his team’s entire total.

Jaynaan managed just **69.24 points**, losing to a team whose other eight starters combined for 37.60. After the previous week’s 70.66-point embarrassment against Holden, Jaynaan had scored **139.90 points across two weeks**.

Tom scored 152.06 this week alone.

Jaynaan had gone from playoff contender to someone who could lose to a spreadsheet with a random-number generator.

Rob did not win this matchup. He simply remained conscious longer than his opponent.

The top three were separating themselves, three managers were fighting for the remaining playoff spots, and Jaynaan was attempting to trade his way out of a crisis that apparently required basic offensive production.

Tom was back. Alastair was surging. Mark was running out of time. Verde was running out of miracles.

And Holden had won two straight.

At this rate, the house might actually get a roof before Jaynaan scores 100 points again.
