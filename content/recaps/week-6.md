Six matchups, one undefeated team left standing, and enough questionable lineup decisions to warrant a league-wide competency review.

## The Battle of the Undefeateds

### Tom 134.28, Roop 76.80

The highly anticipated 5–0 showdown turned into a public execution. Roop entered as one of the league’s juggernauts and proceeded to score 76.80 points, losing by **57.48**.

Five weeks of dominance just to show up to the biggest matchup of the season looking like he had accidentally started his preseason lineup.

## The 1–5 Bowl

### Mark 93.06, Holden 91.24

The closest matchup of the week was between two teams apparently allergic to winning.

Mark **left his defence slot empty**, voluntarily surrendering five points from San Francisco, and still won by 1.82. Holden had now lost five straight games, including one to an opponent who could not even be bothered to field a full team.

> Imagine spending 700 FAAB and making multiple trades just to lose to a guy playing with eight starters.

## The Curse Continues

### Gav 103.48, Verde 100.80

Verde fell to 1–5 despite sitting second in league scoring. Normally, a team with that much offensive production would have accidentally won more than one game by now.

Instead, he lost another heartbreaker by **2.68 points**, adding another chapter to a season where seemingly every opponent found exactly enough points to beat him.

He was no longer playing fantasy football. He was participating in a six-month psychological experiment designed to determine how much disappointment one man could endure before deleting the app.

## The Contender Who Forgot to Contend

### Alastair 93.12, Griff 75.96

Griff entered 4–1, riding a three-game winning streak, and then scored 75.96 against an opponent who barely managed 93.

Alastair even started a player who scored zero and still won by more than 17.

> That is not getting outplayed. That is showing up to a boxing match and knocking yourself unconscious during the introductions.

## The Feeder System Gets Exposed

### Jimmer 112.72, Jaynaan 89.90

Four straight wins had the feeder system looking suspiciously competent until Jimmer decided to ruin the illusion.

Jaynaan left a 20.20-point performance and a 17-point performance on the bench while starting a zero.

Turns out that when your opponents stop donating wins, you actually have to manage your roster. Revolutionary concept.

## The Battle for Mediocrity

### Rob 99.04, Aron 91.40

Rob secured another victory without even reaching triple digits.

Aron needed just 100 points to win and could not manage that, continuing a season where opposing teams had been scoring on him like he was a tackling dummy.

Rob did not win this matchup. Aron simply failed to meet the minimum qualifications for employment.

- **Biggest Ass-Kicking — Tom:** a 57.48-point demolition of the other undefeated team.
- **Biggest Disappointment — Roop:** five weeks undefeated, then 76.80 points in the biggest matchup of the season.
- **Most Embarrassing Loss — Holden:** lost to a team with an empty defence slot.
- **Most Embarrassing Victory — Mark:** won despite apparently forgetting defence was a roster position.
- **Biggest Fraud Alert — Griff:** scored 75.96 after entering the week 4–1.
- **Luckiest Winner — Rob:** 99.04 points was somehow enough.
- **Biggest Statement — Tom:** improved to 6–0 as the league’s highest-scoring team.
- **Most Urgent Intervention Needed — Holden:** five straight losses despite treating the trade market like a 24-hour emergency room.
- **Most Cursed Franchise — Verde:** second in league scoring, 1–5, and another loss by fewer than three points.
