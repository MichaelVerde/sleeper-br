Week 11 punished the standings, the commissioner and anyone who believed a respectable bench score could not hurt them. With three regular-season weeks left, the playoff race finally has teeth.

> **The week in one sentence:** the top seed scored 67, the condemned house won again, and the commissioner somehow found a new basement.

## The Six Autopsies

### Griff 102.90, Roop 67.14

**The throne develops a sinkhole.** The 9–1 league leader arrived looking untouchable and left with the second-lowest score of the week. Griff did not need fireworks: Brock Purdy, Derrick Henry, Breece Hall and DJ Moore simply formed a competent adult committee while Roop’s two starting running backs combined for 7.80 points.

Griff has now won four straight and is tied for second at 8–3. The king is still first, but someone has started measuring the curtains.

### Jimmer 109.80, Tom 92.02

**Dak audits the sovereign wealth fund.** Dak Prescott dropped 35.90, the Texans defence supplied 15, and Jimmer handed Tom a third loss without needing Ja’Marr Chase to do anything useful.

Chuba Hubbard fought back with 26.00 for Tom, but the rest of the portfolio returned 66.02. After spending 965 FAAB, the fund remains very rich, very dangerous and suddenly capable of red days.

### Aron 123.36, Alastair 107.70

**A zero at running back, a win everywhere else.** Rachaad White scored exactly 0.00 and Aron still posted the week’s second-highest total. Ashton Jeanty delivered 25.40, Patrick Mahomes added 24.96, and Tyler Bass kicked in an absurd 18.50 after being picked up for free.

Alastair got 24.70 from CeeDee Lamb and 16.90 from George Kittle, but a zero from the Buccaneers defence helped turn a playoff tune-up into a trap game.

### Mark 94.44, Jaynaan 62.76

**The commissioner has lost the room.** Mark benched Travis Kelce’s 22.40 and still won by 31.68. George Pickens scored 20.30, kicker Dominic Zvada added 17.00, and that was more than enough against Jaynaan, who has now scored **70.66, 69.24 and 62.76** in three consecutive losses.

This is no longer a slump. This is a three-part documentary about administrative collapse.

### Holden 102.90, Gav 84.76

**The condemned property passes inspection.** After one win followed by seven straight losses, Holden has now won three in a row. Emmett Johnson scored 22.30, James Cook added 16.20, and even Cam Little’s 13.40 mattered.

Gav has gone the other direction with five consecutive losses. The house still has no roof, but the city has stopped returning the demolition crew’s calls.

### Rob 122.96, Verde 118.96

**Four points and two bench-shaped knives.** Jonah Coleman exploded for 32.90 and newly acquired Jared Goff added 20.36 as Rob escaped by exactly four. Verde got 25.80 from Jahmyr Gibbs and 19.90 from Mike Evans, then watched Antonio Williams score 16.70 on the bench while Caleb Douglas started for 4.00.

The Bears defence also outscored the starting Bills defence 16.00 to 8.00. Either legal swap flips the result. Hindsight has rarely worn steel-toed boots this well.

## Transaction Desk

### The Goff Recycling Program

Roop dropped Jared Goff. Rob paid 25 FAAB, started him immediately, got 20.36 points and won by four. Roop handed away the player his entire roster identity was built to honour, and that player immediately decided somebody else deserved happiness. Poetry remains undefeated.

### Bass Becomes the Lead Instrument

Aron dropped Xavier Worthy for Tyler Bass and received 18.50 points from the kicker in a 15.66-point win. This roster finally discovered the safest offensive strategy: stop asking the offence to score.

### Commissioner Waiver Theatre

Jaynaan added Seth McGowan, started him, and received 1.30 points. It was the rare transaction that made 62.76 feel meticulously planned.

### Another Structural Renovation

Holden dropped A.J. Brown for Jacory Croskey-Merritt, left Jacory’s 14.70 on the bench, and won anyway. Building codes no longer apply to this property.

## Battle Royale Starts Now

Weeks 12–14 replace head-to-head matchups with three straight weeks of Battle Royale. Opponents no longer matter: the **six highest-scoring teams get a win**, and the bottom six get a loss. After eleven weeks of schedule luck, everyone is finally fighting the whole room at once.

Week 11 provided the perfect dress rehearsal. Verde lost despite posting the third-highest score of the week; under Battle Royale rules, 118.96 would have earned a comfortable win. Mark won his matchup with 94.44, but that score would have landed in the bottom half. Roop’s 67.14 and Jaynaan’s 62.76 would not have had a softer opponent available to save them.

For the next three weeks there are no ugly wins, no noble losses and no hiding behind the one person who happened to be worse. Score in the top six or take the loss. The standings are about to become a weekly public trial.
