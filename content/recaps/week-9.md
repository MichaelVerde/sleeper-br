Nine weeks in, Roop had reclaimed the throne, Tom’s undefeated dynasty had developed a drinking problem, and Holden had finally won another football game.

Meanwhile, Verde was somehow mounting a late-season comeback, and one manager had just paid 100 FAAB to participate in the week’s most embarrassing loss.

## The Matchups

### Roop 136.96, Mark 75.08

A **61.88-point demolition** put Roop at 8–1 and back on top of the league.

Mark managed 75.08, including a five-point running back performance and a 5.60-point starter, while leaving 19.70 points on the bench.

Roop did not even need his best lineup. He could have started a lawn chair at flex and still won comfortably.

The league had a new king, and Mark was apparently invited to the coronation as the court jester.

---

### Verde 113.66, Tom 79.02

The 7–0 dynasty was officially on a two-game losing streak.

Tom spent 965 FAAB building a supposedly unstoppable roster, only to follow the previous week’s 78.98 with **79.02**. That is 158 total points over two weeks. Jimmer scored 174.10 in a single game the week before.

Verde had won two straight after starting 1–6, including a victory against the former undefeated team. The timing could not have been better: Verde had just completed a trade that brought in two players and **100 FAAB**, while Tom had practically nothing left to spend.

One team was experiencing a financial collapse. The other had received a government bailout and immediately used it to overthrow the regime.

---

### Alastair 153.14, Gav 134.46

The week’s best offensive shootout produced a combined **287.60 points**.

Alastair got 29.54 from his quarterback, plus 28.50 and 27.40 from two other starters. Gav answered with a 35.36-point quarterback and a 34.30-point running back—and still lost by 18.68.

After spending 861 FAAB and suffering through weeks of mediocre scoring, Gav finally assembled a legitimate fantasy performance only to run directly into the week’s highest-scoring manager.

He spent two months building a tank, finally got it running, and immediately drove it into an aircraft carrier.

---

### Holden 103.14, Jaynaan 70.66

The seven-game losing streak was over.

Holden finally earned his second win of the season, and naturally it came against a manager who scored **70.66 points**. The real comedy happened before kickoff.

Jaynaan completed a trade with Verde, sending away two players and **100 FAAB** in exchange for two others. Verde then beat the former undefeated manager. Jaynaan proceeded to lose to the league’s most structurally unsound franchise.

Imagine paying another manager 100 FAAB, watching him overthrow the league’s top team, and then becoming the first person in eight weeks to lose to a house without a roof.

Holden had not repaired the house. The neighbours had simply started collapsing around it.

---

### Griff 125.36, Rob 121.78

Finally, an actual competitive football game.

Griff survived by **3.58 points**, thanks in part to a 28-point performance and 25.28 from his quarterback.

Rob had 36.18 from his quarterback and 21.70 from another starter, but also started a player who scored zero while leaving an 18.50-point performance on the bench. That was a potential 18.50-point upgrade in a matchup decided by 3.58.

After spending the early season winning games with scores in the 90s, Rob finally put up 121.78 and lost because his bench apparently had better things to do.

The fantasy gods had finally sent the invoice for all those discounted wins.

---

### Jimmer 95.20, Aron 78.38

Jimmer followed the previous week’s 174-point nuclear strike with a 95.20-point performance and somehow still won.

That made four consecutive victories despite the offence apparently deciding to take the week off.

Aron managed 78.38 while leaving an 18.26-point quarterback performance on the bench instead of his 16.48-point starter. Even that swap would not have been enough.

Jimmer went from dropping a hydrogen bomb to throwing a wet napkin, and Aron still could not defend himself.

## The Trade Desk

**Verde received:** players `13274`, `13301` and 100 FAAB.

**Jaynaan received:** Braelon Allen (`11576`) and player `11834`.

The trade’s long-term winner remained undetermined, but the immediate optics were incredible. Verde won. Jaynaan lost to Holden.

This was the fantasy equivalent of selling your car, giving the buyer $100 for gas, and then watching him drive past while you waited for the bus.

## The Playoff Race

Five regular-season weeks remained: two final head-to-head weeks followed by Battle Royale in Weeks 12–14. Six teams would qualify for the playoffs beginning in Week 15, with points scored carrying enormous weight in any tied records.

### The Front-Runners

**Roop (8–1)** — The new king. Three straight wins, roughly 1,100 points scored, and the inside track to the top seed. The only question was whether this team could avoid peaking before the playoffs.

**Tom (7–2)** — Still comfortably positioned, but two consecutive losses and back-to-back 79-point performances had transformed the league’s final boss into a surprisingly beatable NPC. The 965 FAAB spending spree was starting to resemble a leveraged buyout.

### The Playoff Bloodbath

**Griff (6–3)** — Two straight wins and comfortably in third. Zero FAAB spent all season. Apparently the secret to success was refusing to participate in the league’s collective financial stupidity.

**Jimmer (6–3)** — Four straight wins, including a 174-point explosion followed by a 95-point victory. Nobody knew which version would arrive each Sunday, least of all his opponents.

**Rob (5–4)** — Two straight losses and a disappearing playoff cushion. He had spent 700 FAAB and just lost a matchup that a better lineup might have saved. The bill for those early-season lucky wins had officially arrived.

**Alastair (5–4)** — Coming off a league-leading 153-point week with more total points than several managers ahead in the standings. If this roster got hot at the right time, nobody wanted to draw it in December.

### The Bubble

**Jaynaan (5–4)** — Four consecutive wins earlier in the season had become three losses in four weeks. A playoff contender should not be scoring 70.66 against a building that had already failed its structural inspection.

**Verde (3–6)** — Third-highest in points scored, carrying a positive point differential, and somehow holding only three wins. Two consecutive victories had kept the dream alive, but there was almost no margin for error. The league’s most dangerous manager might not even make the playoffs.

**Mark (3–6)** — Scoring 75.08 while chasing a playoff spot was a bold strategy. He had spent 777 FAAB and was still trying to convince the league that this was a competitive football operation.

### The Long Shots

**Gav (2–7)** — Scored 134.46 and lost. Even when the offence finally worked, the universe apparently refused to cooperate.

**Holden (2–7)** — Finally snapped a seven-game losing streak. Technically still alive, but reaching the playoffs would require a winning streak longer than the one that nearly got the property condemned.

**Aron (2–7)** — Scored 824 points while allowing 1,027. The playoff path required a miracle, several collapses and potentially an investigation into why every opponent turned into the 2007 Patriots.

The biggest story was three managers fighting for what could be only two remaining playoff spots, while Verde desperately tried to turn a top-three scoring offence into an actual postseason appearance.

Someone with a winning record was going to miss the playoffs, and Verde looked capable of finishing with more points than multiple playoff teams while watching from the couch.

If Holden somehow made it, the league would need to commission an independent audit of the entire season.

## Final Verdict

Roop was back on top. Tom’s dynasty suddenly looked like a Ponzi scheme. Verde had discovered winning. Holden had discovered electricity.

Jaynaan had just paid 100 FAAB to help another manager beat the league’s former powerhouse while getting embarrassed by the league’s favourite construction disaster.

Week 9 proved that money cannot buy happiness, but apparently it can buy somebody else’s happiness while you lose to a guy who drafted a doorbell before installing a roof.
