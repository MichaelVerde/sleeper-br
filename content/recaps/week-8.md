Eight weeks in, the last undefeated team finally fell, one manager scored enough points for two teams, and Holden continued proving that you can renovate a house indefinitely without ever making it habitable.

## The Fall of the Dictator

### Jaynaan 101.56, Tom 78.98

Seven weeks, seven wins, 965 FAAB spent and an aura of invincibility. All brought crashing down by a team that scored 101.56 points.

Tom managed a pathetic 78.98, including a zero-point starter and another contributing a single point. Jaynaan left a 15-point performance on the bench and still won comfortably.

Turns out you can buy the entire waiver wire, but you cannot buy a functioning football team every Sunday. The Saudi sovereign wealth fund had suffered its first market correction.

## The Week’s War Crime

### Jimmer 174.10, Gav 79.08

A **95.02-point massacre**. Jimmer put up 174.10 with four starters scoring at least 25, while Gav managed 79.08 despite having already spent 810 FAAB.

Gav’s defence scored zero, his flex scored one, and the entire roster collectively contributed less than half his opponent’s total.

> This was not a fantasy matchup. This was a hydrogen bomb being dropped on a children’s birthday party.

## The City Orders an Evacuation

### Griff 130.78, Holden 88.46

Seven straight losses.

Holden had spent 728 FAAB, traded away half his original roster and was still finding innovative ways to be terrible. This week, he left **22.80 points on the bench** while starting receivers who scored 5.20 and 3.80.

The man spent an eighth-round pick on a doorbell, threw it away, sold the roof, traded the foundation, and was now sitting in the rubble wondering why the property value kept falling.

At this point, the only thing holding the house together was the Sleeper app’s refusal to let him trade himself.

## The Curse Is Temporarily Lifted

### Verde 126.18, Alastair 116.18

After seven weeks of getting repeatedly kicked in the teeth by the fantasy gods, Verde finally managed to score 126 points **and** win a football game.

Alastair put up a respectable 116.18 but apparently forgot that opponents are allowed to have good weeks too.

Verde improved to 2–6 despite scoring nearly 946 points. He was the fantasy equivalent of a billionaire who kept getting mugged on his way to the bank.

## The Return of the Contender

### Roop 125.98, Rob 85.22

After the previous week’s embarrassing 76-point collapse, Roop bounced back with 125.98 and flattened Rob.

Rob managed 85.22, including a zero-point starter and a defence contributing two, despite entering the week on a three-game winning streak.

Rob spent three weeks convincing everyone he was a contender, then showed up to Week 8 looking like he had been assembled from the clearance rack at Canadian Tire.

## The Battle for the Participation Trophy

### Mark 103.90, Aron 85.46

Mark secured his third win despite leaving a **33.60-point performance on the bench**, while one of his starters managed just 2.20.

Aron scored 85.46, somehow making the lineup incompetence completely irrelevant.

One manager tried to sabotage his own team and the other was so terrible he could not capitalize on it. This was not a matchup. It was two people trying to lose an argument.

## Week 8 Awards

- **Biggest Ass-Kicking — Jimmer:** a 95.02-point demolition of Gav.
- **Biggest Upset — Jaynaan:** ended Tom’s undefeated season with just 101.56 points.
- **Worst Bench Management — Mark:** left 33.60 points on the bench while winning with 103.90.
- **Most Condemned Property — Holden:** seven straight losses and still rearranging the furniture.
- **Worst Return on Investment — Gav:** 810 FAAB spent, 79.08 points scored and a 95-point loss.
- **Biggest Reality Check — Tom:** went from 7–0 juggernaut to 79-point punching bag.
- **Most Cursed Franchise — Verde:** finally earned win number two despite remaining among the league’s highest-scoring teams.
- **Biggest Statement — Jimmer:** scored 174.10, won a third straight and suddenly looked like a genuine contender.
